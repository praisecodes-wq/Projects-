Museum obtains new wings for local Artists

The Metropolitan Museum of Art hosted a groundbreaking ceremony Tuesday (15 September) for its Tang Wing for Modern and Contemporary Art, scheduled to open in 2030.

The five-storey, 126,000-sq.-ft space is designed by the Mexican architect Frida Escobedo, the first woman to create a wing for the museum in its 156-year history—a fact that came up often in the morning’s speeches. The Met has already raised $670m to fund building the wing and its operation; it is named for the financier Oscar Tang (a longtime Met trustee) and his wife, the archaeologist and art historian Agnes Hsu-Tang. The couple donated $125m towards the project, the largest cash gift in the museum’s history.

Following a breakfast reception in the museum's Carroll and Milton Petrie European Sculpture Court, the Met’s staff and board joined Escobedo and her team of architects, together with local politicians and neighbours, to celebrate the official start of construction on the Tang Wing. In his introductory comments, the museum's chief executive and director Max Hollein called it the beginning of “the next grand chapter in the history of the Metropolitan Museum of Art”.

“The Tang Wing is not simply more gallery space, but a new way of seeing the art of our time in dialogue with everything else we hold in our collections,” Hollein continued. “The Met is in a moment of great transformation, and the Tang Wing is certainly its most ambitious expression.”

Hollein also highlighted that Escobedo’s design was created to better connect the museum with its surroundings—namely, Central Park and the Upper East Side. “For the first time, the Met will open up to the park, open up to its neighbours,” he said, later adding that the reimagined landscaping around the wing will be “more open, fluid and accessible to the park”.
